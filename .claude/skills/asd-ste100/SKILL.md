# Simplified Technical English (writing rules)

Apply these rules to every sentence you write. Keep every fact from the source. Do not add a fact,
number, technology, employer, or title that the source does not give.

## Sentences
- One idea per sentence. Split a sentence that joins two actions with "and", "while", or a comma.
- Procedural or instruction text: 20 words or fewer per sentence. Descriptive text (resume bullets,
  answers): 25 words or fewer.
- A resume bullet is one sentence. Start it with a past-tense action verb for past work, present tense
  for current work.
- Paragraphs in answers: at most 6 sentences, one topic each.

## Verbs
- Use the active voice. Name who does the action. "I built the API", not "The API was built".
- Use simple verb forms: past, present, future. Avoid "-ing" forms as the main verb.
- Use a verb for an action, not a noun made from a verb: "tested", not "performed testing of".
- Use plain verbs: use, make, build, test, fix, write, reduce, add, move, run, find, start, stop.
- Do not inflate scope: if the source says "helped", "contributed", or "assisted", do not write
  "led", "owned", "architected", "spearheaded", or "drove".

## Words
- Use one term for one thing, and the same term every time.
- Use the concrete noun, not a vague one ("the payment service", not "the solution").
- Keep technical names exactly as the source spells them (product names, languages, frameworks).
- Do not use "it", "this", or "they" when the reader could connect it to two nouns. Repeat the noun.
- Write numbers as digits, with units ("40%", "3 services", "2 weeks").
- No filler: very, really, highly, successfully, effectively, various, numerous, robust (figurative).

## Punctuation
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses.
- No semicolons in bullets. Make two sentences or one shorter sentence.
